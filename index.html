Faculty Defense & Viva Cheat-Sheet
Key takeaway for your presentation: Explain why multi-label classification is required instead of standard single-class classification in civic governance.
1. Multi-Class vs Multi-Label Classification

• Multi-Class: An instance belongs to exactly one class out of many (e.g. Sentiment is Positive OR Negative).
• Multi-Label: An instance can belong to one or more classes simultaneously. For example, a complaint stating "Water pipe cracked and dripping over electrical main box" belongs to BOTH Water Supply AND Electrical Hazard. Traditional single-class systems fail here because they force an arbitrary single label.

2. Why Feature Selection via Chi-Square (χ2)?

In text classification, the vocabulary size (features) can easily exceed thousands of terms, causing the Curse of Dimensionality and overfitting. Feature Selection techniques like Chi-Square (χ2) evaluate the statistical dependency between words and grievance categories, discarding noisy terms (e.g., "today", "yesterday", "please") and retaining high-signal discriminative words (e.g., "pipeline", "spark", "transformer").

3. Evaluation Metrics for Multi-Label Tasks

Standard single-label accuracy is too harsh for multi-label tasks because getting 2 out of 3 labels right shouldn't score 0%. Instead, we use:
• Hamming Loss: Fraction of incorrect label predictions (lower is better; 0 is perfect).
• Micro-F1: Calculates metrics globally by counting total true positives, false negatives, and false positives (preferred when class imbalance exists).
• Macro-F1: Calculates metrics for each category independently and takes the average (gives equal weight to each grievance category).
